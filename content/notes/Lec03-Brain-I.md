# Lec 03 — The Brain I

> One sentence: the mind is what the brain does — and the brain is not a uniform pudding but a set of **specialized, parallel parts**, so damaging one part removes one kind of thinking while leaving the rest intact.

## 1. Driving Question
- Core question: how does a physical organ produce a mind, and is a given mental function **localized** to a piece of tissue or **distributed** across the whole?
- Why it isn't obvious: introspection reports one seamless self, so the naive intuition is that thinking is unitary — either the whole brain does everything (Flourens' equipotentiality), or the mind is non-physical and the brain merely its instrument. Two thousand years of guessing (Aristotle put the mind in the heart; Galen in the fluid-filled ventricles; Descartes in the pineal body because it was the one unpaired structure) shows how weakly intuition constrains this.
- Gap from the previous lecture: [[Lec02-Science-Research]] established what counts as evidence in psychology. This lecture applies that standard to the hardest claim — that mental functions map onto tissue — and the answer turns out to hinge entirely on **method quality**, not on plausibility of the story. Phrenology had the right hypothesis (localization) and worthless evidence.

## 2. Key Concepts (max 5)
| Concept | One-line definition, in your own words | Easily confused with |
|---|---|---|
| Localization of function（功能定位） | A specific mental operation depends on a specific piece of tissue, so removing that tissue removes that operation and little else | Not "one region = one everyday behavior"; phrenology's error was picking the *wrong grain* (traits) not the wrong idea |
| Double dissociation logic（双分离逻辑） | Damage to A kills function 1 but spares 2, damage to B does the reverse → the two functions have separable machinery | A single dissociation (Tan) only shows *involvement*, not that the region is dedicated |
| Lateralization（偏侧化） | Left and right hemispheres run different computations on the same input, not just left/right of the body | Not "left brain logical, right brain creative" pop psychology; the real contrasts are function-vs-appearance and local-vs-global |
| Corpus callosum（胼胝体） | ~200M-fiber bridge letting the two hemispheres share what each has computed | Not a "consciousness generator" — cutting it leaves everyday behavior almost normal |
| Modularity（模块性） | Parts of the brain can process and act on information that the speaking part has no access to | Not unconsciousness — the right hemisphere is doing full-blown perception, it just can't talk |

## 3. Evidence Chain

### Anatomical baseline (why localization is even findable)
- **Design**: descriptive anatomy across levels — molecules → synapses → neurons → networks → maps → systems → CNS.
- **Result**: ~10^12 neurons and ~10^15 synapses; up to 15,000 connections per cell (average ~1,000); ~62,000 miles of myelinated axon; ~100,000 miles of dendrite. One neuron → neuron transmission takes ~10 msec, i.e. **glacially slow** compared with a CPU, yet each neuron does roughly 1,000 multiply-adds per 10 msec.
- **Inference**: because individual elements are slow but astronomically numerous, the brain cannot be a fast serial machine — it must be **massively parallel**. That is the architectural reason to expect different regions doing different things *simultaneously*, which is exactly what the lateralization data later show.
- **Vocabulary that the later evidence rides on**: soma = grey matter; axon = output = white matter; dendrite = input; collection of cell bodies = **nucleus**, collection of axons = **tract**. Lobes: frontal / parietal / temporal / occipital, plus cerebellum, brainstem (pons, medulla), basal ganglia (caudate + putamen = striatum, globus pallidus, subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra), limbic structures (hippocampus, amygdala, hypothalamus). Brodmann (1909) parcellated cortex by **cell architecture alone**, before anyone knew the functions — and his numbered areas later turned out to line up with functional zones (17 = V1, 44/45 ≈ Broca).
- **Weakness**: anatomy constrains but never proves function. Brodmann's map is a hypothesis-generator, not a functional map.

### Phrenology (Gall 1758–1828; Spurzheim 1776–1832) — the negative control
- **Design**: infer mental faculties (amativeness, combativeness, acquisitiveness…) from bumps on the skull; Gall's founding datum was a single soldier whose knife wound penetrated the left frontal lobe below the orbit and who became aphasic.
- **Result**: an elaborate 37-faculty cranial map, sold commercially into the 20th century (the automated "phrenometer", 1907). Flourens' anecdote: Magendie substituted an imbecile's brain for Laplace's, and Spurzheim admired it just as warmly.
- **Inference**: what the anecdote licenses is a claim about the *method*, not about localization. Phrenology failed on three counts — (i) skull shape does not track cortical tissue, (ii) the units were folk-psychological traits rather than information-processing operations, and (iii) there was **no blind measurement**, so the phrenologist's expectation determined the reading. Note that Gall and Spurzheim were **good neuroanatomists** (they correctly described cortex-to-subcortex connectivity, the crossing of the pyramidal tracts, and the commissures) — being right about anatomy bought them nothing about psychology.
- **Weakness / what it does NOT license**: it does not refute localization. This is the lecture's trap: rejecting phrenology and rejecting localization are different moves.

### Phineas Gage (1848) — lesion, personality, orbitofrontal cortex
- **Design**: natural experiment (n=1). Age 25, railroad foreman, described pre-injury as "a shrewd, smart business man… well-balanced mind," the most capable in his contractors' employ. Sept 13, 1848, Cavendish VT: a tamping iron (3 ft 7 in, 1.25 in diameter, 13.25 lb) blew through his head and landed 30 yards away. Damasio et al. (1994) later reconstructed the rod's path through the skull by computer model.
- **Result**: survived; back at work by March 1849; intelligence, language, memory and motor function essentially preserved. But Harlow (1868): "fitful, irreverent, indulging at times in the grossest profanity… impatient of restraint… devising many plans of future operations, which are no sooner arranged than they are abandoned." Friends said he was **"no longer Gage."** Contractors would not give him his job back.
- **Inference**: the observation licenses the conclusion that **social/moral/emotional regulation and planning are dissociable from intellect**, and that they depend on orbitofrontal cortex（眶额皮层）. The force of the case is the *selectivity*: had the damage degraded everything, it would show only that brain tissue matters in general. What makes it evidence for localization is that one faculty fell out while its neighbors stood.
- **What it does NOT license**: (i) that orbitofrontal cortex is *the* seat of morality — necessity is not sufficiency, and a lesion can disrupt a function by disconnecting a network rather than destroying a module; (ii) any quantitative claim, since n=1 with no pre-injury testing and all "personality change" evidence is retrospective testimony from people who knew about the accident. The 1994 reconstruction fixes the anatomy, not the psychology.

### Broca and "Tan" (LeBorgne, 1861) — the birth of the lesion method
- **Prior claims**: Bouillaud argued for left-sided control of speech in 1825; Marc Dax reported in 1836 (published by his son, 1865) a series in which speech disorders followed **left**-hemisphere lesions; Auburtin reported to the Anthropological Society of Paris in 1861 a patient who "had lost his speech but understood everything said to him… his intelligence is still unimpaired."
- **Design**: Broca heard Auburtin speak, received patient LeBorgne five days later — a man who could say only "tan" plus an oath, with right-sided paralysis but otherwise apparently normal. Tan died April 17, 1861; Broca reported the autopsy the next day.
- **Result**: focal lesion in the **posterior third of the third frontal convolution, left hemisphere**. By 1863 Broca had 8 more cases, all left-sided: "I do not dare draw conclusions from this. I await new facts."
- **Inference**: production of speech can be abolished while comprehension and general intelligence remain — a **dissociation** that rules out the equipotentiality view (if cortex were interchangeable, no focal lesion could take out exactly one faculty). And with the accumulation of the left-side series, the *consistency across patients* is what upgrades an anecdote into a localization claim: chance would not put 9/9 lesions on one side. Broca's aphasia（布洛卡失语症）= inability to speak with intact vocal apparatus and normal comprehension; speech is "telegraphic," content words only, complex grammar lost.
- **What it does NOT license**: that Broca's area *is* the language organ. Tan's lesion was large; the correlation is lesion-with-deficit, not region-with-computation. To claim dedication you need the reverse case too (a lesion sparing production but killing comprehension — Wernicke), i.e. a **double dissociation**.
- **Method note (lesion studies generally)**: exquisite for **causal necessity**, hopeless for **anatomical precision** — nature chooses the cut, lesions follow vascular territories rather than functional boundaries, patients are rare and heterogeneous, and the brain has had time to reorganize before you test.

### Wada test — converting a lesion into a temporary, controllable one
- **Design**: sodium amytal injected into one common carotid artery anesthetizes one hemisphere for a few minutes while the patient is tested (typically before neurosurgery).
- **Result**: with the language-dominant hemisphere asleep, speech stops; with the other asleep, it continues.
- **Inference**: this is the cleanest kind of localization evidence available in humans — a **reversible, within-subject** lesion, so each patient is his own control and reorganization has no time to occur. It licenses individual-level statements ("*this* patient's language is left-lateralized") that group lesion studies cannot.
- **Weakness**: invasive, only performed on clinical populations (mostly epilepsy), brief window, and the drug's spread is not perfectly confined.

### Split-brain / callosotomy (Sperry & Gazzaniga tradition)
- **Background**: the corpus callosum was a puzzle. Fechner asked whether a divided brain would mean divided stores of mood and memory; McDougall insisted unity of consciousness does not depend on nervous continuity and volunteered for the operation should he become terminally ill; Erickson (1940) showed the callosum spreads epileptic discharge; and as late as 1957 McCullough could say it "is hardly connected with psychological functions at all," with Lashley joking it was there "to keep hemispheres from collapsing into one another."
- **Design**: patients whose callosum was cut to control intractable epilepsy. Exploit the anatomy: each visual **hemifield** projects to the opposite hemisphere, and each hand is controlled by the opposite hemisphere. Fixate centrally, flash a stimulus to left (LVF→right hemisphere) or right (RVF→left hemisphere) visual field, then ask for a spoken report or a manual response.
- **Results**:
  1. Cup in RVF → says "cup." Spoon in LVF → **reports seeing nothing**, yet the left hand picks out the spoon; asked what it was, says "pencil." Speech production is left-hemisphere in most patients; some comprehension exists in the right.
  2. **Mutual unawareness**: square to LVF, triangle to RVF; the unseen left hand draws a **square** while the mouth says it saw a **triangle**.
  3. **Somesthesis**: no cross retrieval of objects, no cross replication of hand postures, no cross localization of fingertips.
  4. **Different modes of processing the same stimulus**: left hemisphere matches by **function** (scissors→spoon and fork), right hemisphere matches by **appearance** (scissors→needle and thread) — Levy & Trevarthen (1976). With Navon-type hierarchical figures (a big H built of small S's), the left analyzes **local** elements and the right the **global** shape; the same split appears in stroke patients' copies (right-hemisphere damage → the parts survive but the whole is lost; left-hemisphere damage → the outline survives but the parts vanish, Delis et al. 1986).
  5. **The interpreter**: claw to RVF, snow scene to LVF; right hand points to a chicken, left hand to a shovel; the patient explains "I saw a claw and I picked the chicken, and you have to clean out the chicken shed with a shovel." Similarly, "walk across room" flashed to LVF → the patient gets up and walks, and explains "I was thirsty." A nude picture to LVF → blushing and giggling, explained as "Oh doctor, you have some machine!"
- **Inference**: this is the strongest localization evidence in the lecture, and it works because the **stimulus is confined to one hemisphere while the response is demanded from the other**. Reporting "nothing" while the left hand accurately retrieves the object proves the information was fully processed — so verbal report indexes only what the **left** hemisphere knows, not what the person knows. It licenses: (i) speech output is left-lateralized; (ii) the hemispheres are genuinely parallel processors running different algorithms on identical input; (iii) the narrating self **confabulates** causes for behavior it did not generate, which means introspective reports of "why I did that" are unreliable by construction.
- **What it does NOT license**: that consciousness itself splits in two. Everyday unity is usually preserved; conflict cases are exceptions (one hand pulling pants up while the other pulls them down; a patient angrily reaching for his wife with one hand while the other restrained it; "as I reach for something with my right hand, my left comes up and takes something different… I have to call my daughter"; a grocery-store patient shelving an item with one hand and removing it with the other). Nor does it license pop "left-brain/right-brain personality" claims.
- **Weakness**: tiny, self-selected sample of severe-epilepsy patients with abnormal brains and often long-standing reorganization; results vary considerably across the handful of tested individuals; the surgery is sometimes partial.

## 4. Competing Theories
| Position A | Position B | Which evidence discriminates them |
|---|---|---|
| Equipotentiality — cortex is functionally uniform, deficits scale with **amount** of tissue lost (Flourens, and later Lashley's mass action) | Localization — distinct operations sit in distinct tissue | Gage (personality gone, intellect intact) and Tan (production gone, comprehension intact): a *selective* deficit after focal damage is impossible under A |
| Localization at the grain of **faculties/traits** (phrenology) | Localization at the grain of **information-processing operations** | Broca: the deficit respects a processing distinction (produce vs. comprehend) that no folk-psychological trait list contains |
| Callosum is merely structural / seizure-conducting (McCullough, Lashley 1957) | Callosum carries psychologically essential inter-hemispheric information | Split-brain: after section, the same person can simultaneously deny seeing a stimulus and act on it |

Otherwise: — not developed as an explicit opposition in this lecture.

## 5. Levels of Analysis
- Neural/physiological: neuron → network → map → system; grey vs. white matter; Brodmann's cytoarchitectonic parcellation; orbitofrontal cortex, Broca's area, corpus callosum.
- Cognitive/information-processing: massively parallel architecture forced by slow elements; modularity; two hemispheres computing **function vs. appearance** and **local vs. global** on the same input; the left-hemisphere interpreter generating post-hoc explanations.
- Behavioral: aphasic speech; the split-brain response conflicts; copying and drawing tasks after lateralized damage.
- Social/cultural: Gage's story is about **social** competence, not intellect — the lesion cost him his job and his standing, which is how anyone noticed. Phrenology as a cautionary tale about a science becoming a commercial and social technology (hiring, character reading) long before its evidence justified it. Arcimboldo's composite portraits are the same global/local tension used as art.

## 6. Boundaries & Failure Cases
- **Necessity ≠ localization of the whole function.** A lesion tells you a region is needed; it cannot tell you whether the region *computes* the function or merely relays to whatever does.
- **n=1 cases** (Gage, Tan) are historically decisive but statistically worthless in isolation; their force comes from the pattern being replicated (Broca's 8 further cases; Dax's series).
- **Right-hemisphere language exists** in some split-brain patients (comprehension without production), so "language is in the left hemisphere" is a population generalization with real exceptions — the Wada test exists precisely because dominance must be checked patient by patient.
- **Verbal report is a hemisphere-specific instrument.** Split-brain confabulation shows the interpreter will produce a fluent, confident, wrong explanation whenever it lacks the true cause — a general warning about self-report data that reaches far beyond neurology.
- Split-brain patients look normal in daily life; the deficits appear only under lateralized stimulus control. So a null result in ordinary observation says nothing.

## 8. Self-Test (close the notes first)
1. To prove to a non-psychologist that mental functions are localized, I'd cite the split-brain cup/spoon test: the patient *says* he saw nothing while his left hand picks the right object. The persuasive move is that both facts are true of one person at one moment, which no "the mind is one thing" account can absorb.
2. Rebuttal to "of course brain damage changes you, that's common sense": common sense predicts **graded, global** decline — more damage, worse everything. Gage kept his intelligence, memory, language and motor skill and lost only social judgment; Tan kept comprehension and lost only production. Selectivity, not severity, is the datum.
3. Transfer: if a patient reliably names objects placed in the right hand but not the left, and shows no visual deficit, the prediction is a callosal (or left-hemisphere sensory-to-language) disconnection rather than an object-knowledge deficit — testable by having the left hand *point to* the object among alternatives, which should succeed.

## 9. Open Questions
- Given Gage's rod destroyed tissue *and* connections, how would one ever separate "orbitofrontal cortex computes social judgment" from "orbitofrontal cortex is a hub the computation passes through"? (Lec04's methods partly answer this.)
- Broca's area shows up in imaging for non-linguistic sequencing too — so is the deficit in Broca's aphasia about *language* or about *structured sequential output*? Not addressed here.
- Why is speech left-lateralized at all, given both hemispheres perceive language to some degree? The lecture reports the fact without a mechanism.
- What is the left-hemisphere interpreter for, functionally, if its explanations can be flatly false?
